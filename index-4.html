EAGLE EYEElectrical cabinet quality inspection

We’re a team of four students exploring how computer vision and simple data checks could help with electrical cabinet inspections. The idea is to compare what a cabinet photo shows with what the engineering list says should be there.

01 / What we’re building

A second pair of eyes for QC.

Checking every label by hand can be repetitive. EAGLE EYE is meant to organize that first pass: read visible labels, compare them with a reference list and flag anything that needs a closer look.

It’s an inspection aid, not an automatic safety certificate. A person still needs to verify uncertain results and carry out the required electrical checks.

What the prototype aims to do

  • ✓Read visible labels from cabinet photos (OCR)
  • ✓Load expected component names from Excel or PDF
  • ✓Compare expected and detected labels
  • ✓Mark results as PASS, FAIL or REVIEW
  • ✓Show results in a simple web interface

03 / The workflow

How the pieces fit together

The goal is to keep the process straightforward and easy to explain during the demo.

STEP 01

Load the reference

Read the expected component list from an Excel sheet or supported document.

STEP 02

Check the photo

Try to locate and read component labels using image processing and OCR.

STEP 03

Compare labels

Match the detected text against the reference list and flag uncertain cases.

STEP 04

Review the result

Show the findings in a table so an inspector can verify them.

PASS The available evidence supports a match.

FAIL A discrepancy is confirmed under the comparison rules.

REVIEW The evidence is incomplete or uncertain.

A label not read by OCR doesn’t prove a component is physically missing. Glare, blur, viewing angle and blocked labels can all affect the result.
